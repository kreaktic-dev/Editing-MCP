# "The Tower That Refused to Stand Up" — original script v1

Facts (Wikipedia "Leaning Tower of Pisa"; leaningtowerofpisa-guide.com history + facts pages; EBSCO research starter):
- Construction began 1173. In 1178 the tower began to sink once building reached the second floor: a foundation only
  ~3 m deep in weak, soft subsoil (clay, sand).
- Construction then stopped for almost a century (1178-1272) because Pisa was at war with Genoa, Lucca and Florence.
  The pause let the soil settle and consolidate; otherwise the tower would almost certainly have toppled.
- From 1272 the builders made the upper floors taller on one side to compensate, so the tower is curved.
- In the 1930s (1934) Mussolini called the lean an embarrassment and ordered it straightened; ~90 tonnes of concrete
  were injected into the foundation. The tower sank further and the lean increased.
- By 1990 the lean was 5.5 degrees; the tower was closed on 7 January 1990. 1993-2001: 38 m3 of soil removed from under
  the raised (north) side; the tilt was reduced by 45 cm (to its 1838 position, ~3.97 deg). It was never meant to be fully
  straightened; the lean is the attraction.

## Voice-over (read in one steady tone, ~3.7 words/s, ~60 s)

Builders in Pisa spent two hundred years finishing a tower that started falling over after five.
It's eleven seventy-three, and Pisa wants the fanciest bell tower in the whole world.
So the builders dig a foundation just three meters deep, straight into soft, wet clay.
By the second floor, the whole thing is already tipping over.
Luckily, Pisa goes to war, and everybody drops their tools for almost a hundred years.
That accident saved it. The mud underneath finally had time to squeeze tight.
When they come back, they try to hide the lean by building the new floors taller on the low side.
So now the tower isn't just leaning. It's bent, like a banana.
In nineteen thirty-four, Mussolini calls it embarrassing and orders it straightened, so his engineers pump ninety tons of concrete into the base.
It leans even further.
You see, the ground under Pisa is like a mattress. Put more weight on one side, and that side just sinks deeper.
By nineteen ninety, it was so close to falling that Italy shut it down, and engineers quietly pulled soil out from under the high side.
The tower swung back forty-five centimeters, and they stopped.
They could have made it perfectly straight. But nobody flies to Italy to hold up a tower that's standing up by itself.
